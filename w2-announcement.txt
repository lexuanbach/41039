WHAT TO EXPECT IN THE LECTURE

Week 1 gave you state — a program that runs top to bottom and does the same
thing every time. These two weeks add the other two tools, and that is nearly
everything:

  * Branching, so a program can choose:  if / else if / else, and switch,
    in Java and in Python.

  * Repetition, so a program can do something a thousand times without you
    typing it a thousand times:  while, for, for-each, do-while, break and
    continue.

  * Then the structures worth repeating over: arrays in Java, and lists,
    dictionaries, sets and tuples in Python.

Two honest warnings, because they catch nearly everyone:

  - Arrays and lists are numbered from 0, not from 1. The last element of a
    10-element array is number 9. Almost every array bug you will write in your
    life is one step either side of that.

  - A loop that never changes its condition never stops. Every loop you write,
    ask yourself: what makes this stop?

Both have a whole section of the website page to themselves.

IF YOU ONLY DO ONE THING

On the website page, predict the output of every runnable block before you press
Run. Being wrong and finding out one second later is the fastest form of learning
available to you, and it costs nothing.

And as always: ask early, ask on the Ed forum, and ask publicly unless it is
genuinely personal. Everyone gets stuck learning to program. The students who do
well are not the ones who never get stuck — they are the ones who ask.

The forum is not monitored over the weekend, so if something is broken, ask
during the week rather than on Sunday night.
